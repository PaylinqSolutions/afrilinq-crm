1. Overview

Afrilinq CRM is one connected platform for Afrilinq Technologies. It covers the whole customer lifecycle: capturing and converting leads, managing accounts and contacts, running a deal pipeline, a mandatory Sales → Onboarding handover when a deal is won, onboarding projects, customer training, a support desk with SLA clocks and escalations, customer success (health, renewals, expansion), quotes, invoices and SLA agreements, a knowledge base, tasks, notifications, reports and a full audit trail, all behind role-based access control.

Every module shares one relational database. A customer's record therefore shows its contacts, deals, handover, onboarding, training, tickets, documents, activities, files and audit history together.

Status. Version 1 is built and runs locally with demo data. This document describes the behaviour in the code so engineering can maintain and extend it. Items marked Later are not built.

2. Problem and goals

Problem

  • Customer information is spread across spreadsheets, inboxes and chat, so nobody sees the full picture of an account.
  • Deals are won without the details Onboarding needs. Promises made in sales get lost, and go-lives slip.
  • Support has no consistent priority rules or SLA tracking, and escalations to other teams happen informally.
  • Leadership can't see pipeline, onboarding risk, support load and customer health from one source.

Goals

  1. One record per customer, linked across sales, onboarding, training, support and success.
  2. A won deal can't reach Onboarding without a complete, reviewed handover.
  3. Support priority and SLA clocks follow written rules, not tone, and respect business hours and holidays.
  4. Each role sees exactly the records it should, enforced on the server.
  5. Every important change is audit-logged and every report drills down to the records behind it.
  6. Concurrent edits never silently overwrite each other.

4. Customer journey and how modules connect

Lead→Contact / Company→Deal→Closed Won→Handover→Onboarding→Training→Live→Customer success
  • Leads are unqualified enquiries. Converting one always creates or reuses a contact. A company and a deal are optional.
  • Companies are the account everything attaches to. Contacts are its people, or individuals with no company.
  • Deals belong to a company, involve contacts and carry product lines from the product catalogue.
  • Winning a deal makes the company a customer and creates exactly one handover. Accepting the handover starts exactly one onboarding project from the product's template.
  • Training sessions are scheduled for the company, usually tied to that project. Required programs count towards go-live readiness.
  • Tickets are always raised on an existing company or contact. Escalations hang off tickets.
  • Quotes, invoices and SLA agreements are raised for a company and recipient contact, optionally from a deal.
  • Customer success tracks health, renewals and expansion for every company whose lifecycle is "customer".
  • Every detail page shows the customer journey tracker and a Related records panel limited to modules the viewer can open.

5. Modules

Each module lists its purpose, what it does, the rules the server enforces, and its routes. The User stories tab has acceptance criteria for each.

Leads Built

Capture enquiries from any source, qualify them and convert the good ones.

  • Lead list with owner, status, source and idle flags; create, edit, assign (leads.assign).
  • Organisation is optional, so individuals can be captured.
  • Convert lead: always produces a contact; company optional (pre-selected when one with the same name exists); "Also open a deal" needs a company. Logged activity moves to the contact.
  • Duplicate guard: before creating a contact the server matches by email (case-insensitive), then phone (last 10 digits, formatting ignored), then full name at the same company. It reuses the match and fills only blank fields, unless the user ticks "create a separate contact".
  • The sweep flags idle leads.

Contacts Built

The people Afrilinq sells to and supports.

  • Workspace with My / Unassigned / All tabs, saved views, filter chips (owner, create date, last activity, lead status) and advanced filters (lifecycle, source, has email/phone/company, city).
  • Data-quality cards: missing owner, email, lead status or phone; no activity in 30 days; new in 30 days.
  • Inline editing of every cell with conflict checks. The phone editor normalises numbers (0803 481 9348 → +234-803-481-9348) or keeps them as typed.
  • Column picker, sort, 25/50/100 paging, a lead-status board, bulk assign/status/lifecycle/export/delete with undo, duplicate finder, quick view.
  • Record page: log Note, Email, Call, Task, Meeting, WhatsApp, SMS, LinkedIn or postal mail with optional follow-up task; Overview, Activities and Intelligence tabs; sidebar with deals, colleagues, tickets, training, onboarding, documents and attachments.
  • Logging a call moves an untouched lead status to Connected or Attempted to contact.

Companies Built

The account record that every other module attaches to.

  • List mirrors Contacts: All / My / Customers tabs, filters (owner, dates, lead status, lifecycle, segment, tier, region, industry, city, has contacts, has open deals), summary cards, inline edit, lifecycle board, bulk actions, duplicate finder (similar names or same domain).
  • Record: editable firmographics (domain, industry, owner, CSM, lifecycle, segment, tier, phone, address, employees, annual revenue, time zone, LinkedIn, record source), activity logging, Overview / Activities / Intelligence tabs, journey tracker, related records.
  • Customer 360 view with billing, custom fields, handovers and audit history.
  • Soft delete only (deleted_at), with data.delete and audit.

Deals and pipeline Built

Track opportunities from first conversation to Closed Won or Lost.

  • Kanban board and table, with drag-and-drop stage moves and a quick-view panel.
  • Ten stages with probabilities: New Opportunity 10%, Discovery 20%, Qualified 30%, Demo Scheduled 40%, Demo Completed 50%, Proposal / Quote Sent 60%, Negotiation 70%, Verbal Commitment 85%, Closed Won 100%, Closed Lost 0%. Each stage has entry and exit criteria, required fields (admin-configurable) and a stale threshold.
  • Every open deal needs a next step and due date.
  • Closed Won gate: value, primary contact, at least one product, signed agreement or approval reference, billing frequency, start date, and no unapproved discount. The deal page and board quick view show a readiness checklist linking to each missing item.
  • Closed Lost requires a loss reason.
  • Discounts above the threshold (default 10%) need Sales Manager approval (deals.approve_discount). Commercial fields lock after Closed Won.
  • Stage history records time in each stage; forecast category comes from probability (≥70 commit, ≥50 best case).

Activities and tasks Built

  • Activities (call, email, meeting, demo, proposal, note, WhatsApp, SMS…) log against a contact, company, deal or lead, can be pinned, and appear on every related timeline.
  • Tasks have a stage (Not started, In progress, Waiting, Completed, Deferred), type, priority, queue, due date and assignee. Views: Due today, Overdue, Upcoming, All open, Completed and saved views; stage board; bulk complete/reschedule/reassign; "Start N tasks" to work through a list.
  • A task created on a record shows on the Tasks page and stays on the record's timeline; editing it in either place updates both.
  • Assigning notifies the assignee; completing someone else's task notifies its creator.

/tasks

Sales → Onboarding handover Built

  • Moving a deal to Closed Won creates exactly one handover (UNIQUE(deal_id), idempotent), pre-filled from the deal. Sales, their manager and the onboarding queue are notified; the deal shows "Handover incomplete".
  • Sales completes a 20-item checklist (17 required): customer and contacts, products and scope, agreement reference, pricing, start date, discount, goals, current processes, every commitment Sales made, technical readiness, training needs, target go-live and why, documents and risks.
  • Submitting creates a draft onboarding project once. Onboarding accepts or requests changes, with a comment required either way.
  • Accepting an incomplete handover needs handover.override plus a reason, and is audit-logged with the missing fields.
  • After acceptance Sales is read-only; corrections go through request and approve, keeping the old value in the audit log.
  • The Onboarding board shows a "Coming from Sales" list of handovers not yet accepted.

/handovers · /handovers/[id]

Onboarding Built

  • Exactly one project per handover (UNIQUE(handover_id)). On acceptance the matching template (by product, then segment) creates dated tasks, milestones, dependencies and a go-live checklist.
  • 15 stages from Handover Review to Onboarding Completed, plus On Hold / At Risk (reason required).
  • Risk is rule-based and shows every reason: overdue milestones (>7 days → high), open high/critical blockers, go-live date passed, and required training gaps inside the configured window before go-live.
  • Task dependencies block starting or completing a task.
  • Completion is an explicit confirmation with go-live date and closure notes (onboarding.complete). Anything outstanding needs an exception reason, which is audit-logged.

Training Built

  • Programs by product, role and segment, with objectives, materials (customer or internal) and assessments.
  • Sessions link to the company, onboarding project and participants (contacts). Week calendar, list and programs views.
  • Attendance: Attended, Absent, Rescheduled, Cancelled. Completion, competency and retraining flags per participant; a failed assessment flags retraining.
  • Required programs feed onboarding risk and go-live readiness.
  • Trainers see only sessions they facilitate and only the participants they need.
  • Reminders are in-app; email reminders need a provider.

Support desk and SLA Built

  • Tickets must be raised on an existing company and/or contact; a requester email matching a contact links it. Eleven queues; table and board views.
  • Statuses: New, Triaged, Assigned, In Progress, Waiting on Customer, Waiting on Engineering / Product, Waiting on Third Party, Resolved (pending confirmation), Closed, Reopened.
  • Priority = impact × urgency, not tone. A documented workaround caps P2 at P3. Suspected security, data exposure, outage or data loss is always P1, opens an incident and notifies the Support Lead, executives and admins. Manual override needs tickets.priority_override plus a logged reason.
  • SLA policies (selected by customer tier) hold time zone, hours, workdays, holidays, per-priority targets, warn/escalate thresholds and which statuses pause the clock. Pauses record who and why and extend due dates by paused working time.
  • The automatic acknowledgement doesn't count as first response unless an admin enables it.
  • Resolving needs a summary and category; P1/P2/incidents also need a root cause, or "Root cause not yet known" plus a follow-up owner (creates a task). Reopening snapshots the old resolution.
  • Likely duplicates are flagged and must be dismissed. Routing picks a team by category or tier and the least-loaded agent.
  • Internal notes live in a separate table and never reach customer views.

/tickets · /tickets/new · /tickets/[id] · /settings/sla

Escalations Built

  • From an open ticket, escalate to Engineering (team), another support agent, the Support Lead, Sales, Onboarding or Management.
  • Flow: raise → approval (agents' Engineering and Management escalations) → accept → internal updates → resolve → requester confirms. Also reassign, reopen, withdraw and decline (reason required).
  • Engineering escalations require environment, reproduction steps, expected and actual result, customer impact and business consequence. Engineering can record status, root cause and fix version; "fix released" or "won't fix" resolves automatically.
  • Response targets: P1 4h, P2 24h, P3 72h, P4 120h. Receivers can see the escalated ticket even outside their normal scope.
  • /escalations inbox with tabs and a navigation badge. A separate escalations PRD and clickable prototype exist.

Customer success Built

  • Transparent weighted health score from onboarding, support load, overdue actions, training completion, engagement recency and CSAT. Weights and thresholds are editable and each component shows its reason.
  • "At Risk" accounts create an account-review task. Renewals inside the notice window create renewal-review tasks.

Billing and agreements Built

  • SLA agreements built from the customer's tier and SLA policy (targets table frozen at creation) with 13 editable standard clauses.
  • Quotes (can copy a deal's products and prices; VAT from settings) and invoices (from an accepted quote or blank). Numbering SLA/QUO/INV-YYYY-0001.
  • "Mark as sent" creates a private client link /d/[token]. The client views, downloads a PDF (browser print), signs or accepts online (typed name, title, authorisation box; IP and time recorded) or declines with a reason.
  • Paper signatures: upload the signed PDF; it's attached to the company's files.
  • Payments recorded against sent invoices (part or full; overpayment refused). Overdue and expired statuses come from due and validity dates.
  • Company billing details (legal name, RC, TIN, bank, signatory) live in Settings.
  • Online payment links Later need a payment provider.

6. Cross-cutting requirements

AreaRequirement
Access controlEvery service call checks a permission and applies a row scope (src/server/services/scope.ts): e.g. sales executives see their own leads and deals, agents their team and assigned tickets, engineering only escalated tickets, trainers only their sessions. Record-level denials return 404 so existence isn't revealed; module denials show 403.
AuditRole and permission changes, financial and contract fields, deal stage and amount, discounts, ticket priority and status, handover decisions and overrides, onboarding completion and exceptions, settings, SLA policies, exports and deletions.
Edit conflictsEdit forms carry the updated_at they loaded. Writes are compare-and-set (UPDATE … WHERE updated_at = loaded). A conflicting save is rejected with who changed what and when. Append-only actions (replies, notes, activities) never conflict.
Data integrityForeign keys everywhere, unique constraints for "exactly one" rules, soft delete on customer records, timestamps on every table.
SchedulingrunAllSweeps() every 60s (or POST /api/cron/sweep with a bearer secret on serverless): SLA warn/escalate/breach once per clock cycle, idle leads, stale deals, overdue tasks, onboarding risk, renewals, health.
SecurityUploads checked by extension, MIME and magic bytes, 10 MB cap, random names outside public paths, served only through an authorised route. Security headers (frame deny, nosniff, referrer and permissions policy). Markdown escapes HTML. Server action origin checks. No secrets in the repo.
LocalisationNaira by default with ISO currency per document; Africa/Lagos time zone, configurable; Nigerian phone normalisation; en-GB dates.
UI statesEvery page has loading, empty, error, not-found and forbidden states; forms show field-level errors. Works at desktop and phone widths. Back arrow on every screen.
No fake successNothing is shown as sent or connected unless it was. Sends without a provider need an explicit "simulate" and are labelled Simulated, not delivered.
TestingVitest unit tests for pure rules and service-level acceptance tests against an in-memory Postgres (87 tests passing). Browser E2E suite Later.

7. Architecture

  • Stack: Next.js 16 App Router (server components and server actions), React 19, TypeScript strict, Tailwind v4, Drizzle ORM, Zod, Recharts, lucide-react, Vitest.
  • Database: Postgres 14+ in production (e.g. Supabase via DATABASE_URL); PGlite (Postgres in WASM) locally. Migrations in /drizzle run on start.
  • Request flow: page → ctx() resolves the actor → service checks permission and scope → Drizzle query. Mutations go through act(), which maps domain errors to field errors.
  • Layout: src/db (schema, seed), src/lib/rules (pure, unit-tested business rules), src/server/services (all business logic and authorisation), src/app/actions (thin server actions), src/app/(app) (staff screens), src/app/portal and src/app/d (public pages).
  • Environment: DATABASE_URL, APP_URL, CRON_SECRET, STORAGE_DIR, SWEEP_INTERVAL_SECONDS, AUTO_SEED, plus optional integration credentials.

8. Integrations

Email, SMS, WhatsApp, calendar, telephony and issue tracker are optional adapters behind one interface, configured by environment variables only. Status is Not configured until variables exist and Connected only after a registered adapter passes a test. No provider adapters ship yet. In-app notifications always work.

9. Out of scope and known limitations

  • No real email, SMS, WhatsApp, calendar, telephony or issue-tracker providers.
  • No inbound email-to-ticket; no customer portal login (per-ticket links and help centre only).
  • Custom fields on companies only; one pipeline in the UI (the model supports more).
  • Assessments record trainer-entered scores; no in-app quiz.
  • No online payments; PDFs come from the browser's print dialog; e-signatures are typed-name with an audit trail, not certificate-based.
  • Postgres RLS not used (scope enforced in the data layer); per-instance rate limiting.

10. Next phase and open questions

  1. Email provider adapter (invitations, ticket replies, CSAT, document sending).
  2. Payment provider (Paystack or Flutterwave) for invoice payment links and automatic reconciliation.
  3. Playwright E2E suite for the critical paths.
  4. Issue-tracker sync for engineering escalations.
  5. Production hardening: shared rate limiter, object storage, optional Postgres RLS.

Open questions for the business

  • Confirm pipeline stages, probabilities and the 10% discount threshold.
  • Confirm required handover fields, onboarding templates and required training per product.
  • Confirm SLA targets, business hours and public holidays per tier.
  • Data retention periods for closed tickets, audit logs and files.
