1. Summary

Support agents regularly need help from people outside the support queue: Engineering for defects, a more experienced colleague, the Support Lead, the customer's Sales account owner, the Onboarding owner, or management. Today this happens informally in chat and email, so nobody can see who owns the problem, how long it has waited, or whether it was actually fixed.

This feature adds a structured escalation on any open ticket. The flow runs raise → (approve) → accept → update → resolve → confirm, with reopen, reassign, withdraw and decline paths. Response targets come from severity, every step notifies the right people, and an Escalations inbox shows each person what is waiting on them.

2. Problem

  • No ownership. Requests to other teams are sent in chat. They get lost, and the customer waits without anyone being accountable.
  • Poor engineering hand-offs. Engineering receives vague requests ("customer says it's broken") and has to come back for basics, which adds days.
  • No closure loop. When another team "fixes" something, support often isn't told, or the fix isn't checked with the customer before the matter is dropped.
  • No visibility for leads. Support Leads can't see how many escalations are open, overdue or waiting for approval.
  • Cross-team access. People outside support often can't open the ticket they are being asked to help with.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. Any authorised support user can escalate an open ticket to one of six targets in under a minute.
  2. Every escalation has one clear owner (a person or a team) and a response due time.
  3. Engineering escalations arrive with enough technical detail to start work without follow-up questions.
  4. Nothing is closed until the requesting side confirms the fix with the customer.
  5. Support Leads control which escalations reach Engineering and Management.
  6. Everyone sees their escalation workload in one inbox.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Syncing with an external issue tracker (Jira, Linear, GitHub). Only a reference field is kept.
  • Customer-visible escalation status. Escalations are internal; customers are updated through the ticket conversation.
  • Automatic escalation by rules (e.g. auto-escalate P1 after 2 hours). The SLA sweep already raises the ticket's escalation level separately.
  • Email or Slack delivery of notifications. v1 uses in-app notifications only.

5. User stories

  1. As a support agent, I want to escalate a ticket to a named colleague, so the right expert owns my question.
  2. As a support agent, I want to send a defect to Engineering with reproduction steps and impact, so engineers can start immediately.
  3. As a Support Lead, I want to approve or decline agent escalations to Engineering and Management, so those teams only get qualified requests.
  4. As an engineer, I want to see escalations assigned to my team and post status updates, so support knows where the fix stands.
  5. As a receiver, I want to accept an escalation, so the requester knows someone has it.
  6. As a receiver, I want to pass an escalation to a better-placed colleague with a note.
  7. As a requester, I want to be told when it's resolved and confirm or reopen after checking with the customer.
  8. As a requester, I want to withdraw an escalation I no longer need.
  9. As a sales account owner, I want commercial escalations about my accounts to come straight to me.
  10. As anyone, I want a single inbox, with a count in the navigation, of what is waiting on me.

11. Screens

  • Ticket page → Escalations panel: an "Escalate" form that adapts to the target, plus an escalation card per escalation with a progress strip and role-based actions.
  • Escalations inbox (/escalations): tabbed list with ticket, customer, target, severity, owner, due time and status. Each row links to the ticket's escalation panel.
  • Navigation: "Escalations" item with a count badge.
  • Notifications: each notification links straight to /tickets/[id]#escalations.

13. Edge cases and rules

  • A ticket can have several active escalations at once (for example Engineering and Sales).
  • Closing or resolving the ticket doesn't close its escalations; they stay visible until confirmed or withdrawn. The exception is incident escalations, which resolve and reopen with the incident ticket.
  • If the Sales account owner or Onboarding owner is missing or inactive, the user must pick a person.
  • If an assignee is deactivated, a lead reassigns the escalation. Leads can always act as receivers.
  • Declines and withdrawals require a reason so the other side understands why.
  • Reopening a confirmed escalation is allowed and is counted in reopen_count.
  • Incident-type escalations (from the incident flow) use an approved status that behaves like open.

14. Out of scope and future work

  • Two-way sync with Jira, Linear or GitHub issues (the external_ref field is ready).
  • Email and Slack/Teams notifications, and reminder nudges when the due time passes.
  • Escalation reporting dashboard (ESC-23).
  • Rules that auto-escalate, e.g. P1 tickets with no response in N minutes.
  • Linking several tickets to one engineering escalation for widespread bugs.

15. Open questions

  1. Should Sales escalations about discounts or contract changes also need Sales Manager approval?
  2. Should unconfirmed resolutions auto-close after a set period (e.g. 5 business days) if the requester doesn't respond?
  3. Do response targets need to respect business hours, like ticket SLAs, rather than calendar hours?
  4. Should the customer ever see "escalated to our engineering team" in the portal?
  5. Who owns the PRD and success metrics: Head of Support or Product?
