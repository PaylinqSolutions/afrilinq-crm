How to escalate a ticket

Bring in Engineering, a colleague, Sales, Onboarding or management when a customer issue needs more than you can resolve alone, then follow it through until it is fixed and confirmed.

Escalations always start from a ticket. There is no stand-alone "new escalation" page, so every escalation stays tied to the customer's ticket, company and contact.

Raise an escalation

  1. Open the ticket. Go to Tickets and open the customer's ticket. The ticket must still be open; closed or resolved tickets can't be escalated.
  2. Find the Escalations section. Scroll down the ticket page to Escalations and click Escalate.
  3. Choose who to escalate to. Pick a target in Escalate to (see the table below). Depending on your choice you'll pick an engineering team or a person.
  4. Set the severity. P1–P4 sets the response target for whoever receives it. It defaults to the ticket's priority.
  5. Say what you need. In What do you need from them? write the question, what you've already tried, and what the customer is waiting for.
  6. Submit. Click Raise escalation. The receiver is notified straight away, or a Support Lead is asked to approve it first.

Response targets

The severity you choose sets how quickly the receiver should respond. The due time shows on the escalation and in the inbox.

P1within 4 hours
P2within 1 day
P3within 3 days
P4within 5 days

What happens next

Awaiting approval→ Waiting to be accepted→ In progress→ Resolved — awaiting confirmation→ Resolved and confirmed

Approval only applies to Engineering and Management escalations raised by agents. Other outcomes: Declined and Withdrawn.

  1. Approval (Engineering and Management only). A Support Lead clicks Approve or Decline. A decline needs a comment, which is sent back to you.
  2. Accept. The receiver gets a notification and clicks Accept. You and the ticket owner are told who picked it up.
  3. Work and updates. Either side can Add update. Updates are internal and never shown to the customer. Engineering can also record a status (investigating → identified → fix in progress → fix released / won't fix), root cause and fix version. "Fix released" or "won't fix" resolves the escalation automatically.
  4. Resolve. The receiver clicks Mark resolved and explains the fix. For Engineering, a ticket that was waiting on engineering moves back to In progress.
  5. Confirm. You (or the ticket owner) are notified. Check with the customer, then click Confirm resolved. If it isn't fixed, click Reopen and say what's still wrong. It goes back to the receiver.

Other actions

Reassign

The receiver can pass the escalation to another person with a note. The new owner is notified and must accept it.

Withdraw

If you no longer need help (say the customer found a fix), withdraw it with a reason. The receiver is notified.

Decline

A Support Lead can decline an escalation awaiting approval. A reason is required.

Reopen

Available after resolution if the problem returns. The reopen count is tracked.

Tips

  • Be specific in the reason. "Customer can't export invoices since Monday's update; tried clearing cache and a second browser" gets faster help than "export broken".
  • Keep the customer updated from the ticket's conversation. Escalation updates are internal only.
  • You can't escalate to yourself, and you can only pick active users.
  • People you escalate to can see the ticket even if it's normally outside their queue.
